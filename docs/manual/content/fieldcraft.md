# Fieldcraft

The game is supposed to kill you. A clean win is a thing you remember with your hands, not a thing the numbers hand you. These are the habits that survive contact with the later dungeons.

Aim before you enter. Hold Aim means the last diagonal is still true when you stop to shoot. Set it in the doorway. Firing north because you forgot you were aimed northeast is a classic way to donate a heart to a sentry.

Dash is a resource, not a walk. The invulnerable moment is short. Spend it through a shot, not through empty floor. Double-tapping because you are nervous is how you arrive in the next pattern with the dash already gone.

B is free and it is on a clock. Use Howl to delete a fan, not to open a room that Fang Forms would have cleared. Use Stoneskin when you have already chosen the tile you will stand on. A ward in the wrong tile is a statue. Raven Mark is one target. Pick the summoner, the bell, the thing that makes the room worse by existing. Swarm and Tidal Wave are commitments. Do not press them as a flinch.

Will is the attack you were patient for. Release A while you move. The charge hates a shield and it hates Mute, and it does not care that you are in a menu. At speed 5 the tiers take longer as they climb. Faster champions charge faster, up to a cap. Do not hoard tier 4 for a boss if tier 2 would have saved the room that was about to end the run. A stored Will that you die holding is worth zero.

Spirit Convergence is the full meter, not a panic button you can afford twice. It empties magic, and magic is also how you pay for an Oath and for bombs. Go into a colossus with a full meter if you can. Spend Convergence on the crisis, the last quarter, the moment the pattern stops being the pattern you learned.

Oaths are unlocked by wins, in order. Shard Wake is an eight-way answer to a crowded first room. Root Call pulls bodies into one knot and does not stun them. You still have to survive what you gathered. Cinderstep is a short lunge along your aim. Still Wave slows hostile shots instead of deleting them. Miasma chips everything and finishes nothing. Gloam Shift moves you along your aim onto legal ground. Sun Turn rotates the live bullets and can throw a switch. Red Harvest nicks up to five foes and returns a sliver of health if Hunger is not on you. Rift Exchange swaps you with a nearby body that is not huge. Read that list once. Then use the one the room is asking for, not the one you unlocked last.

Buy the tool that matches the death. Hookshot and Rift Hook are different objects. One pulls you. One lets you cross a hole, and in Shadow Keep that is the difference between a run and a record in the fallen column. The Boomerang is the button you press while B is cooling down: it stops ordinary bodies, cuts shots, and fetches the heart you were too proud to walk toward. It does not care about a colossus. Do not throw it at one and feel betrayed.

The village is part of combat. Fill health and magic before north. Thirty coins on a Boomerang will save more runs than thirty coins on a luck relic you do not understand yet. The chart is for when the Compass is a rumor. The forge’s attack is permanent for the run and boring and correct.

On Normal, do not expect the game to top you off. Stay above half if you can, and know that an alpha will pay you in coins instead of a heart while you are comfortable. That is intentional. Easy is for learning the shapes. It is not a secret true ending. There is no Hard. If Normal feels unfair, it is often because a diagonal was still loaded.

Cracks are amber in every biome. If the wall is amber, shoot it. If a vault says it will not refill, believe it. Take the gift before you leave.

And when you fall, the title is telling the truth. Only knowledge persists. The next vessel can be a different one. The Serpent will still coil. You will know when to flee it.
